Packs

Recipe packs ready to install

A pack is a recipe book you install with one tap from Settings › Recipe packs. Its recipes can't be edited, but you can copy any of them to your books.

  • Official catalogue: Miga reads this website's catalogue; each pack has a cover, an author and a description.
  • Other catalogues: add another one with a URL or a GitHub “user/repo”.
  • Make your own: export a book as a Miga file (ZIP) and describe it in a catalog.json.

World cuisine

World cuisine: United States

American home cooking, from the South to New England: mac and cheese, pulled pork, fried chicken, clam chowder, chili, cornbread, pancakes, meatloaf, jambalaya, gumbo, coleslaw, apple pie, chocolate chip cookies, New York cheesecake, cheesesteak, Cobb salad and key lime pie. With measurements in grams.

World cuisine: Argentina

From the asado to afternoon tea: beef short ribs, chimichurri, salsa criolla, choripán, provoleta, criolla empanadas, milanesa a la napolitana, locro, matambre, humita, carbonada, gnocchi with tuco, pastel de papa, revuelto gramajo, dulce de leche, alfajores, pastafrola, medialunas, tortas fritas and chipá.

World cuisine: China

Classics from Sichuan, Cantonese and northern Chinese cooking: kung pao chicken, mapo tofu, jiaozi, sweet and sour pork, hot and sour soup, wontons, chow mein, hong shao rou, dan dan noodles, char siu, steamed fish, spring rolls and congee. With the basic sauces and spices of the Chinese pantry.

World cuisine: Colombia

Cooking from Colombia's regions: bandeja paisa, Antioquian beans, ajiaco, sancocho, arepas, empanadas, patacones, hogao, ají, arroz con pollo, changua, pandebono, buñuelos, natilla, posta negra, mote de queso, coconut rice, seafood casserole, sobrebarriga and coconut lemonade. With substitutes for the ingredients that are harder to find outside Colombia.

World cuisine: France

The classics of French bistro and home cooking: beef bourguignon, coq au vin, quiche Lorraine, croque monsieur, gratin dauphinois, bouillabaisse, cassoulet, blanquette de veau, moules marinières, duck breast with orange, hachis parmentier, cheese soufflé, Lyonnaise salad, pissaladière, tapenade and, for dessert, tarte Tatin, crème brûlée, profiteroles, cherry clafoutis and madeleines. With butter, wine and herbs.

World cuisine: Germany

German classics from north to south: schnitzel, rouladen, sauerbraten, Swabian potato salad, Bratkartoffeln, Kartoffelpuffer, Käsespätzle, Königsberger Klopse, currywurst, Frikadellen, lentil and split pea stews, potato soup, Flammkuchen, pretzels, Maultaschen, Obatzda and sweets such as apple cake, Black Forest gateau and rote Grütze.

World cuisine: Greece

Greek cooking beyond the salad and tzatziki: souvlaki, spanakopita, pastitsio, gemista, fasolada, avgolemono, dolmades, keftedes, briam, fava, kleftiko, stifado, saganaki cheese, prawns with feta, gigantes, taramasalata, melitzanosalata, spanakorizo, loukoumades and baklava. With olive oil, lemon, oregano and dill.

World cuisine: India

Curries, pulses, breads and sweets from India: butter chicken, tandoori chicken, rogan josh, korma, vindaloo, palak paneer, chana masala, dal tadka, rajma, aloo gobi, baingan bharta, samosas, onion bhajis, raita, mint chutney, chapatis, aloo paratha, kheer, mango lassi and masala chai.

World cuisine: Indonesia

Everyday and festive dishes from Indonesia: nasi goreng, mie goreng, chicken satay with peanut sauce, gado-gado, rendang, soto ayam, opor ayam, bakso, sayur lodeh, sambal and sweets such as pisang goreng, martabak and klepon. With substitutes for the less common ingredients.

World cuisine: Italy

The great classics of Italy's regions: carbonara, amatriciana, cacio e pepe, lasagne, pasta alla Norma, spaghetti with clams, risotto alla milanese, ossobuco, saltimbocca, parmigiana, minestrone, pasta e fagioli, pizza margherita, focaccia, arancini, vitello tonnato, caponata, puttanesca, orecchiette with turnip tops, gnocchi alla sorrentina, chicken cacciatore, ribollita, panzanella, carpaccio, calzone, tiramisu, panna cotta, cannoli and cantucci. Made the Italian way, with no cream in the carbonara.

World cuisine: Japan

Japanese home cooking: miso soup, chicken teriyaki, gyoza, ramen, tonkatsu, oyakodon, onigiri, tamagoyaki, okonomiyaki, yakitori, tempura, maki rolls, karaage, nikujaga, yakisoba, gyudon, Japanese curry and dorayaki. With dashi and the basic sauces explained step by step.

World cuisine: Korea

The cooking of Korea, spicy, fermented and made for sharing: bibimbap, bulgogi, homemade kimchi, japchae, kimbap, tteokbokki, kimchi and soft tofu stews, samgyetang, seafood pajeon, kimchi fried rice, dakgalbi, Korean fried chicken, galbi jjim, bossam, side dishes (banchan) such as rolled omelette, seasoned bean sprouts and cucumber salad and, for dessert, hotteok and patbingsu. Gochujang and gochugaru are sold in Asian shops.

Mediterranean cooking

Recipes from around the Mediterranean: Spain, Italy, Greece, Lebanon, Morocco and Tunisia. Gazpacho, paella, Spanish omelette, moussaka, hummus, falafel, shakshuka, risotto, tagine and more, with olive oil, seasonal vegetables and pulses.

World cuisine: Mexico

Mexican classics to cook at home: tacos al pastor, cochinita pibil, pozole, chilaquiles, enchiladas, salsas, guacamole, aguas frescas and flan. With easy-to-find ingredients and alternatives where needed.

World cuisine: Morocco

The cooking of Morocco, with gentle spices and slow simmering: seven-vegetable couscous, harira, chicken pastilla, tagines of lamb with prunes, kefta with eggs and fish, zaalouk, salads, bissara, briouats, rfissa, mechoui and kefta skewers, breads such as msemen, khobz and baghrir, honey and almond sweets and mint tea.

World cuisine: Peru

Peruvian cooking from the coast, the Andes and the chifa tradition: ceviche, tiradito, lomo saltado, ají de gallina, causa limeña, papa a la huancaína, ocopa, arroz chaufa, anticuchos, pollo a la brasa, rocoto relleno, seco de res, arroz con pollo, chupe de camarones, papa rellena, tallarines verdes, suspiro limeño, picarones, mazamorra morada and chicha morada. With substitutes for Peruvian chillies.

World cuisine: Spain

A tour of Spain's regional cooking: cocido madrileño, fabada, lentil stew, marmitako, bacalao al pil pil, garlic chicken, black rice, fideuà, oven-baked rice, escalivada, Rioja-style potatoes, Galician broth, papas arrugadas with mojo, ajoblanco, Galician empanada, migas, oxtail stew, torrijas, rice pudding and Santiago almond cake. Without repeating the dishes in the Mediterranean, tapas and Christmas packs.

World cuisine: Thailand

Thailand's balance of hot, sour, sweet and salty: pad thai, tom yum, tom kha gai, green, massaman and panang curries, som tam, larb, fried rice, pad kra pao, pad see ew, cashew chicken, beef salad, fish cakes, Thai omelette, khao man gai, gai yang, jaew dipping sauce, mango sticky rice and Thai iced tea. With substitutes for the less common ingredients.

World cuisine: Turkey

The cooking of Turkey, from Istanbul to Anatolia: breakfasts of menemen and çılbır, lahmacun, pide, börek, simit, mantı, İskender and Adana kebabs, chicken şiş, karnıyarık and imam bayıldı aubergines, bulgur pilaf, kısır, mercimek köftesi, ezme, shepherd's salad and sweets such as künefe and revani, with ayran to drink.

World cuisine: Vietnam

Vietnam's fresh, fragrant cooking, from Hanoi to Saigon: phở, bún chả, bánh mì, fresh and fried spring rolls, bún bò Huế, cơm tấm, caramelised fish and pork, sour soup, shaking beef, lemongrass chilli chicken, bánh xèo, chicken salad, bún thịt nướng, nước chấm, tofu in tomato sauce and, for dessert, banana in coconut milk, iced coffee with condensed milk and avocado smoothie.

Fusion cuisine

Fusion cuisine: Anglo-Indian

Dishes born where India and Britain met, from the clubs and trains of the Raj to British curry houses: kedgeree, mulligatawny soup, chicken tikka masala, coronation chicken, jalfrezi, railway mutton curry, country captain and mango chutney.

Fusion cuisine: Chifa

The cooking of Cantonese immigrants in Peru, which spread from Lima's Chinatown to the whole country with the wok, soy sauce and Peruvian produce: tallarín saltado, chi jau kay, kam lu wantán, aeropuerto, pollo enrollado, fried wontons, pork with tamarind and fuchifú soup.

Fusion cuisine: Chinese-American

The cooking Chinese immigrants created in the United States, the food of the takeout box: General Tso's chicken, orange chicken, beef and broccoli, chop suey, egg foo young, crab rangoon, Mongolian beef and lo mein.

Fusion cuisine: Creole

The Creole cooking of New Orleans, where France, Spain, West Africa and the Caribbean met: shrimp Creole, red beans and rice, crawfish étouffée, grillades and grits, dirty rice, muffuletta, beignets and bread pudding with whiskey sauce.

Fusion cuisine: Indo-Chinese

Indo-Chinese cooking, born among the Hakka Chinese of Kolkata and now at every street stall in India: gobi manchurian, chilli chicken, Hakka noodles, Schezwan fried rice, chilli paneer, manchow soup, sweetcorn soup and honey chilli potatoes.

Fusion cuisine: Italian-American

The cooking of Italian immigrants in New York, New Jersey and San Francisco, more generous with meat, cheese and sauce than Italy's: spaghetti and meatballs, chicken parmesan, penne alla vodka, fettuccine Alfredo, baked ziti, shrimp scampi, cioppino and Italian wedding soup.

Fusion cuisine: Italian-Peruvian

What 19th-century Ligurian immigrants left on the Peruvian table, mixed with Andean chillies, potatoes and cheeses: tallarines rojos, tallarines a la huancaína with lomo saltado, menestrón, chard pie, yellow potato gnocchi and panetón.

Fusion cuisine: Japanese-Chinese

Chūka ryōri, the Japanese-style Chinese cooking of the Chinatowns of Yokohama and Nagasaki and of neighbourhood restaurants: chāhan, tenshinhan, ebi chili, ebi mayo, hiyashi chūka, Nagasaki champon, shūmai and nira reba.

Fusion cuisine: Korean-Chinese

The dishes of Korea's Chinese restaurants, created by Shandong immigrants in Incheon and ordered for delivery all over the country: jajangmyeon, jjamppong, tangsuyuk, kkanpunggi, lajogi, yusanseul, gun-mandu and goguma mattang.

Fusion cuisine: Malaysian Chinese

The cooking of Malaysia's Chinese (Hokkien, Cantonese, Hainanese and Hakka) at the hawker stalls of Penang and Kuala Lumpur: char kway teow, bak kut teh, Hokkien mee, yong tau foo, claypot chicken rice, wantan mee, Hainanese chicken chop and kaya toast.

Fusion cuisine: Peranakan

The Nyonya cooking of the Peranakan, descendants of Chinese and Malays in Malacca, Penang and Singapore, which blends Chinese techniques with spices, coconut and tamarind: laksa lemak, asam laksa, ayam pongteh, ayam buah keluak, otak-otak, kueh pie tee, chap chye and kuih dadar.

Fusion cuisine: Tex-Mex

The cooking of Texans of Mexican descent, with more yellow cheese, cumin and beef than Mexico's: nachos, chile con queso, cheese enchiladas with chili gravy, crispy beef tacos, breakfast tacos, King Ranch chicken, Frito pie and sopaipillas.

Everyday cooking

20-minute dinners

Home-made dinners on the table in 20 minutes or less, with ingredients from any supermarket: omelettes, scrambled eggs, pasta, griddled fish, chicken stir-fry, vegetable soups, hearty salads and quick pizzas. For the days when you don't feel like a fuss.

Bakery

Bread and pastries to make at home, from sourdough starter to madeleines: sourdough loaf, baguette, sandwich loaf, wholemeal, rye, chapata, walnut and raisin bread, pitta, naan, bagels, burger buns, milk rolls, brioche, croissants, pains au chocolat, ensaïmadas, tortas de aceite, Spanish magdalenas and anise rosquillas. With exact weights and proving times.

Biscuits and cakes

Cakes for breakfast and biscuits for the afternoon, homemade: orange cake, marble cake, carrot cake, lemon and poppy seed cake, apple cake and coffee cocoa cake, and oat and raisin, ginger, lemon and cinnamon biscuits, butter tea biscuits and langues de chat. With notes on keeping and freezing them.

Budget cooking

Complete dishes with cheap, seasonal ingredients, the home cooking of a lifetime: green beans with potatoes, battered potatoes in sauce, vegetable rice, tuna and tomato spaghetti, chicken and potato stew, Flamenco eggs, tuna empanadillas, baked mackerel, liver and onions, French onion soup, cauliflower with garlic and paprika and broccoli pasta. With money-saving tips in the notes.

Cooking with kids

Simple recipes to cook as a family, with jobs children can do on their own: oat and banana cookies, coconut balls, yoghurt ice lollies, strawberry milkshake, tuna stuffed eggs, pasta salad, chicken wraps, sandwich-bread pizzas, mini quiches, baked meatballs, salmon parcels and chocolate salami. Each recipe says what the child can do; the oven, sharp knives and the hob always with an adult.

Five-ingredient recipes

Dishes with five ingredients or fewer (not counting salt, pepper, oil or water), for when the fridge is nearly empty or you can't be bothered: poor man's potatoes with egg, baked salmon with lemon, beetroot hummus, baked prawns, ham and cheese stuffed chicken, spaghetti al pomodoro, scrambled eggs with prawns and green garlic, Galician-style hake, Brazilian cheese bread and, for dessert, banana and cocoa ice cream, peanut butter cookies and strawberries and cream.

Use-it-up cooking

Recipes so nothing goes to waste: what is left of a Spanish stew, yesterday's roast chicken, stale bread, fish from dinner, vegetables going soft and fruit that is too ripe. Ropa vieja, stew croquettes, roast chicken lasagne, roast meat cannelloni, empanadillas, mash and roast meat pie, baked eggs with leftover vegetables, fish loaf, ripe fruit smoothie and jam, vegetable peel stock and croutons.

No-bake desserts

Desserts made in the fridge or a saucepan, without turning on the oven: no-bake cheesecake, triple chocolate cake, lemon icebox pie, lemon mousse, tocino de cielo, yoghurt and berry pots, date and cocoa balls, cereal bars, chocolate hazelnut spread, strawberry fridge cake, mango and yoghurt pots and pears in red wine. Most are made the day before.

Packed lunches for work

Meals that keep well in the fridge, travel in a lunchbox and reheat without losing their charm: bean and quinoa salads, chicken and rice, roast chicken thighs with potatoes, chickpeas with chorizo and pumpkin, chicken with mushrooms, macaroni with chorizo, burritos, stir-fried noodles, potato and spinach omelette, potato and tuna pie, bulgur with vegetables, chicken pesto pasta and stuffed peppers. With notes on how to keep and reheat each one.

Rice dishes

Paellas, dry, creamy and soupy Spanish rices and rice dishes from around the world: seafood, mixed and vegetable paella, arroz del senyoret, arroz a banda, rice with rabbit and snails, crusted rice, soupy lobster rice, creamy mushroom rice, arroz empedrado, Murcian garden rice, Cuban-style rice, pilaf, biryani, pumpkin risotto, gallo pinto, arroz con gandules and jollof. And there is no chorizo in any paella.

Kitchen appliances

Air fryer

Recipes made for the air fryer: chips, wings, home-made nuggets, breaded fish, crispy vegetables, meatballs, burgers, ribs and even desserts. With very little oil and the time and temperature for each dish. Times are a guide: every model heats differently.

Microwave

The microwave is good for much more than reheating: mug cake, baked eggs with ham, salmon and cod with vegetables, cheese flan, risotto, broccoli with almonds, lemon chicken, stuffed aubergines, express berry jam, chocolate custard and candied almonds. Times for an 800 W microwave: shorten them with more power, lengthen them with less. Use microwave-safe dishes and cover with a microwave lid or vented cling film.

Pressure cooker

Long stews in half or a third of the time with the pressure cooker: Madrid-style tripe, beef and potato stew, pork knuckle, ox tongue in sauce, pig's trotters, chicken in beer, larded beef, Asturian beans with clams, pinto beans with chorizo, cuttlefish with potatoes, soupy chicken rice and garlic rabbit. Times are counted from when the cooker reaches pressure (second ring or high pressure). Never fill it more than two-thirds full, or half full with pulses.

Oven: sheet-pan dinners

Complete dinners made on a single oven tray: prepare, put it in and forget it, with only one thing to wash up. Mustard chicken with vegetables, salmon with potatoes and asparagus, sausages with vegetables, chicken fajitas, gnocchi with cherry tomatoes, pork chops with potatoes, turkey with sweet potato, whole roast cauliflower, hake with boulangère potatoes, tray pizza, vegetables with halloumi and chicken with chorizo and chickpeas.

Thermomix: baby food

Homemade purées and porridges for starting solids, from 6 months, programmed for the Thermomix TM6 (they work just the same on the TM5 and TM7): vegetables, chicken, beef, white fish, pulses, fruit and oats. No salt or sugar. Every baby goes at their own pace: always follow your paediatrician's advice on introducing foods and textures, and offer one new food at a time to spot allergies.

Thermomix: breads and doughs

Breads and doughs kneaded in the Thermomix TM6, which work just the same on the TM5 and TM7 (on a TM31, knead with the dough function): oat sandwich loaf, country loaf, pizza dough, braided milk bread, spelt bread, beer bread, olive bread, flour tortillas, breadsticks, chicken and pepper empanada, crêpes, leek and bacon quiche, chocolate chip buns and Asturian chorizo buns. The Thermomix kneads; the oven does the rest.

Thermomix: desserts

Homemade desserts programmed for the Thermomix TM6, which work just the same on the TM5 and TM7: Spanish custard, crème caramel, fried custard squares, lemon curd, chocolate mousse, lemon sorbet, express strawberry ice cream, yoghurt cake, creamy baked cheesecake, chocolate truffles, strawberry jam, pastry cream, butter biscuits, walnut brownie and Spanish biscuit cake. On a TM31, use 90°C where 85°C is given and check the texture before finishing.

Thermomix: sauces and basics

The sauces and basics used most in the kitchen, programmed for the Thermomix TM6, which work just the same on the TM5 and TM7: béchamel, mayonnaise, aioli, sofrito, homemade tomato sauce, basil pesto, bolognese sauce, cheese sauce, brava sauce, hollandaise, romesco, herb yoghurt sauce, mushroom sauce and vegetable and chicken stocks. Many of them freeze well in portions.

Thermomix: soups and purées

Smooth soups and purées programmed for the Thermomix TM6 (2.2 litre bowl), which work just the same on the TM5 and TM7. Pumpkin, mixed vegetable, vichyssoise, mushroom, carrot and ginger, broccoli, asparagus, pea and mint, tomato, red lentil, cauliflower, spinach, sweet potato, artichoke, roasted pepper, chestnut, celeriac, white bean, beetroot and mashed potato. On a TM31 (2 litre bowl, 100 °C maximum), fry at Varoma temperature.

Thermomix: stews and one-pot dishes

Home-style stews programmed for the Thermomix TM6 (2.2 litre bowl), which work just the same on the TM5 and TM7: lentils with rice, chickpea and salt cod stew, white beans with chorizo, potatoes with pork ribs, beef with mushrooms, chicken in almond sauce, chicken curry, salt cod in tomato, turkey stew, mixed vegetable stew, prawn risotto, Spanish broth with egg and ham, pork cheeks in red wine, squid with potatoes and chicken in tomato. On a TM31 (2 litre bowl, 100 °C maximum), fry at Varoma temperature and scale the quantities down a little.

Healthy cooking

Healthy cooking: batch cooking

Healthy dishes to cook in one afternoon and eat all week: they keep well in the fridge or freezer and reheat without losing anything. Chicken and vegetable traybake, lentils with vegetables, spinach with chickpeas, turkey chili, turkey meatballs, ratatouille, quinoa salad, salmon with sweet potato, chicken curry with chickpeas and sweet potato tacos.

Healthy cooking: breakfasts and snacks

Breakfasts and snacks with oats, fruit, yoghurt, nuts and eggs, with little added sugar: apple porridge, overnight oats, homemade granola, chia pudding, oat and banana pancakes, banana muffins, oat and date bars, green smoothie, ricotta and strawberry toast and spinach scrambled eggs.

Healthy cooking: gluten-free

Dishes without wheat, barley or rye, made with rice, corn, pulses, buckwheat and nuts: chickpea socca, asparagus risotto, almond-crusted salmon, cauliflower-crust pizza, buckwheat galettes, polenta with mushrooms, courgette lasagne, meatballs in almond sauce, salt-baked sea bream and flourless chocolate cake. Always check the labels of stocks, sauces and spices.

Healthy cooking: light and filling

Dishes under 450 kcal per serving that really fill you up, with lean protein, vegetables and fibre: hake en papillote, niçoise salad, chicken skewers with yoghurt, chicken and vegetable soup, baked cod with tomato, prawn stir-fry, turkey stuffed with spinach, chilled cucumber soup, chicken and courgette burgers and chicken, mango and avocado salad.

Healthy cooking: low salt

Recipes with no added salt or very little, that get their flavour from herbs, spices, garlic, citrus and vinegars, and without very salty ingredients such as cured meats, aged cheeses or soy sauce: lemon-marinated chicken, sea bass with fennel and orange, pork tenderloin with apple, leek and apple soup, brown rice salad, spiced roast vegetables, courgette omelette, salt-free seeded bread, homemade tomato sauce and baked pears.

Healthy cooking: vegan

100% plant-based, complete dishes, with pulses, tofu, wholegrains and nuts so you don't fall short on protein: tofu and broccoli stir-fry, lentil bolognese, chili sin carne, quinoa and chickpea bowl, roasted cauliflower tacos, lentil burgers, vegetable and coconut curry, rice noodle salad with tofu, white bean stew and chocolate avocado mousse.

Healthy cooking: vegetarian

Dishes without meat or fish, with eggs, dairy, pulses and plenty of vegetables, complete in protein: vegetable frittata, aubergines stuffed with quinoa and feta, peppers stuffed with brown rice, black bean burgers, lentil salad with feta, crustless spinach quiche, courgettes stuffed with ricotta, cauliflower baked with yoghurt, couscous with vegetables and halloumi and baked eggs with spinach.

Specials

Barbecue

Recipes for a summer party around the barbecue or grill: Iberian pork burgers, ribs, chicken and vegetable skewers, Iberian pork secreto, lamb chops, grilled salmon and sardines, corn on the cob, foil-baked potatoes, grilled vegetables, homemade barbecue sauce, tomato and tuna belly salad and, for dessert, grilled pineapple and bananas. Almost everything also works on a griddle or under the oven grill.

Christmas special

Timeless recipes for Christmas Eve, Christmas Day and Twelfth Night: prawn cocktail, ham croquettes, galets soup, roast lamb, suckling pig, sea bream, hake in green sauce, red cabbage, roscón de Reyes, turrón, polvorones and marzipan. With notes for cooking ahead and for big gatherings.

Halloween

Recipes for a Halloween party, with pumpkin and plenty of fun to make with the kids: pumpkin and sweet potato soup, sausage mummies, spiderweb pizza, spider deviled eggs, witch fingers, banana ghosts, clementine pumpkins, graveyard brownies, candy apples, pumpkin bread, spiced pumpkin cookies and witch's brew punch. Ask about allergies, and take care with hot caramel and whole olives around little ones.

Kids' birthday party

Everything for a children's birthday party, easy to make ahead: chocolate cake, rainbow cake, vanilla cupcakes, decorated biscuits, mini pizzas, mini sandwiches, baked chicken fingers, sausage rolls, mini burgers, fruit skewers, popcorn and homemade lemonade. Always ask families about allergies and intolerances, and avoid whole nuts and popcorn for under-fours because of the choking risk.

Holy Week in Spain

The recipes of Lent and Holy Week in Spain: fried sweets such as pestiños, cream puffs, rosette fritters and bartolillos, the Easter mona cake and the hornazo, and the Lenten salt cod dishes (Biscay-style, soldaditos de Pavía, fritters, omelette, potatoes with cod and cod and raisin empanada). Torrijas and the Lenten chickpea stew are already in other packs.

Spanish tapas

Bar tapas from all over Spain to share: patatas bravas, Padrón peppers, Galician-style octopus, marinated anchovies, fried squid rings, shrimp fritters, mushroom croquettes, chorizo in cider, pinchos morunos, huevos rotos, Russian salad, gildas and pan con tomate, among others.
